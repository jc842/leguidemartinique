# Martinique Island Complete Guide: 34 Communes, Volcanic North & Golden South

Known as "Madinina" (the Island of Flowers), Martinique offers a striking geographical contrast between its wild, mountainous volcanic northern peaks and its sun-soaked southern bays.

---

## 1. The Volcanic North: Primary Rainforest & Mount Pelée
Recognized as a UNESCO World Heritage site, the northern massif features rugged peaks, black volcanic sand beaches, and deep river gorges:
- **Saint-Pierre**: The "Little Paris of the Antilles" destroyed by the 1902 eruption of Mount Pelée, now an atmospheric City of Art and History.
- **Le Morne-Rouge & Fonds-Saint-Denis**: High-altitude rainforest hamlets surrounded by giant ferns, hanging heliconias, and roaring cascades like Saut-Gendarme.
- **Grand'Rivière**: A dramatic fishing outpost situated at the northernmost road's end, facing the Dominica channel.

## 2. The Caribbean South: White Sands & Tranquil Bays
The flatter southern region is the hub of seaside relaxation:
- **Les Anses-d'Arlet**: A picturesque fishing community famous for its church facing the sea and sea turtles grazing in shallow seagrass beds.
- **Sainte-Anne & Les Salines**: Iconic golden crescent beaches fringed by curved coconut palms and turquoise Caribbean waters.
- **Le Diamant**: Overlooked by the towering 175-meter volcanic basalt monolith of Diamond Rock, renowned for scuba diving and maritime history.

## 3. The Atlantic Coast & Caravelle Peninsula
- **Tartane**: A historic fishing and surf hub located on the Caravelle Peninsula Natural Reserve, home to the ruined 18th-century Château Dubuc.
